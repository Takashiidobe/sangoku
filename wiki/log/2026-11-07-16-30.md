# Linking real core into a ROM (sangoku-30k.9.1.8)

_2026-11-07 16:30_

## Context

Each crate lowered into one RGBDS section per codegen unit: `core` about 3.2 MB,
`compiler_builtins` 4.7 MB, the 771-function driver 507 KB at O0, against a 32
KiB ROM0 limit. `rgblink` cannot drop unused parts of a section, and opt levels
`s`/`z` barely changed size. `sangoku-30k.9.1.8` (P1) asked for per-function
sections, a reachability pass from `gbc_main`, and ROMX banking.

## Case

`tests/core_rom/rom.rs`: iterator `filter`/`sum`/`max` over a byte array, linked
with real `core`.

## Before

No `core`-using program could be assembled and linked into a ROM.

## After

104 of 12,255 sections survive, 16,136 bytes. That exceeds the 16 KiB of ROM0
left after header and runtime, so the rest goes to ROMX bank 1.
`tests/core_link.rs` links and runs the ROM in the emulator and checks
`gbc_main` returns 28; the emulator already handled everything `core` emitted.

## How

The backend emits one section per function (named after the symbol) instead of
per codegen unit; the cgu header and `rgbds::new_source` are gone and the lower
fixtures regenerated. `tools/link_core.py` compiles `core`, `compiler_builtins`
and the program to assembly, splits the text into sections, keeps those
reachable from `__rust_gbc_main` by following any symbol token another section
defines, and assembles the result. A probe link puts everything in ROMX, reads
section sizes from the map, fills ROM0 free space first and moves the rest to
ROMX bank 1; a no-MBC 32 KiB cartridge maps both banks at once so ordinary calls
work, and `rgblink -t` rejects anything larger.

Also found: the panic symbol `core` references is the v0-mangled std-internal
name `_RNvCsbOwsw4zHH1Z_7___rustc17rust_begin_unwind`, so the earlier
`runtime/panic.asm` label (plain `rust_begin_unwind`) would never have resolved;
fixed and the test follows. Not pushed.

## Alternatives

Not done: MBC banking with cross-bank calls for programs over 32 KiB, and wiring
this into `sangoku build`.
