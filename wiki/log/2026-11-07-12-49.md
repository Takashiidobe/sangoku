# Audit 16-bit pointer assumptions without core changes (sangoku-30k.7)

_2026-11-07 12:49_

## Context

The target has 16-bit `usize`/`isize` and pointers, but `core` and `alloc` have
paths written for at least 32-bit `usize`: slice length limits, `Layout`,
`size_of` checks, `Range<usize>` arithmetic, hashing and const evaluation.
`sangoku-30k.7` (P0) asked to check them and fix upstream-visible bugs in the
fork or document them. Acceptance: `core` and `alloc` build for the target with
no `usize`-width cfg workarounds, plus fixtures for maximum slice and array
sizes.

## Case

Unmodified upstream `core`, `alloc` and `compiler-builtins` compiled for
`targets/lr35902.json`, plus fixtures at the 16-bit limits.

## Before

Unaudited; no fixtures covered the limits of a 16-bit address space.

## After

All three crates compile target metadata with no `usize`-width workarounds, and
no Rust-fork or backend changes were needed. Positive fixtures
(`tests/fixtures/core/pointer-width.rs`) cover 32767-byte ordinary objects,
65535-element zero-sized arrays and slices, alignment-aware `Layout` limits,
zero-sized `Vec` capacity, checked arithmetic, native hash-byte width and
endianness, and `Step` range bounds. Negative fixtures reject a 32768-byte array
(`oversized-array.rs`) and a 65536-valued `usize` literal
(`oversized-usize.rs`).

## How

Fixtures run through `tests/core.rs`; bounded Rust-to-emulator tests in
`tests/lowered.rs` check `usize` wrapping arithmetic and raw pointer offsets at
opt `none`/`speed`. Findings are in `wiki/concepts/16-bit-pointers.md`. All 116
nextest tests, clippy, fmt, LSP diagnostics and the blank-ROM smoke pass. Not
pushed.

## Alternatives

Upstream `ExactSizeIterator` has a known caveat for overlong wider and inclusive
ranges; upstream behavior is preserved and documented rather than patched with
fork cfgs. Not done: full core/alloc machine-code emission, linking and heap
execution, which remain in `sangoku-30k.9`.
